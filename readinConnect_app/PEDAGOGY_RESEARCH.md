## 1. Phonics Pedagogy

### Research-Informed Approach

#### Phoneme Progression (Jolly Phonics-Inspired)

Based on systematic phonics research, phonemes should be taught in this order:

**Phase 1: Group 1 (Most Common)**
- /s/ (s, ss) - "s" in "sun"
- /a/ (a) - "a" in "cat"
- /t/ (t) - "t" in "tap"
- /p/ (p) - "p" in "pat"
- /ɪ/ (i) - "i" in "pig"
- /n/ (n) - "n" in "nap"

**Phase 2: Group 2**
- /k/ (c, k, ck) - "c/k" in "cat"
- /e/ (e) - "e" in "pen"
- /h/ (h) - "h" in "hat"
- /r/ (r, rr) - "r" in "rat"
- /m/ (m) - "mm" - "m" in "map"
- /d/ (d, dd) - "d" in "dog"

**Phase 3: Group 3**
- /g/ (g, gg) - "g" in "goat"
- /o/ (o) - "o" in "pot"
- /u/ (u) - "u" in "cup"
- /l/ (l, ll) - "l" in "leg"
- /f/ (f, ff) - "f" in "fun"
- /b/ (b, bb) - "b" in "bat"

**Phase 4: Group 4**
- /æ/ (ai) - "ai" in "rain"
- /j/ (j) - "j" in "jam"
- /oa/ (oa) - "oa" in "boat"
- /i:/ (ie) - "ie" in "pie"
- /eɪ/ (ee) - "ee" in "see"
- /z/ (z, zz, s) - "z" in "zip"

**Phase 5: Group 5**
- /w/ (w) - "w" in "wet"
- /ŋ/ (ng) - "ng" in "sing"
- /v/ (v) - "v" in "van"
- /ʊ/ (oo) - "oo" in "moon"
- /ju:/ (y) - "y" in "yes"
- /ks/ (x) - "x" in "box"

**Phase 6: Group 6**
- /ʧ/ (ch) - "ch" in "chip"
- /ʃ/ (sh) - "sh" in "shop"
- /θ/ (th) - "th" in "thin"
- /ð/ (th) - "th" in "this"
- /kw/ (qu) - "qu" in "queen"
- /ou/ (ou) - "ou" in "out"

#### Letter Sound vs. Letter Name

**Critical Distinction:**
- Letter NAME: "A" (how you say the letter in alphabet)
- Letter SOUND: /æ/ (how it sounds in words like "cat")

**Example:**
- Letter "a" name: "A" (like "ay")
- Letter "a" sound: /æ/ (short vowel, like in "cat")
- Letter "a" sound: /eɪ/ (long vowel, like in "cake")

**Teaching Approach:**
1. Always teach letter SOUND first, not name
2. Show letter → play sound → child identifies
3. Add letter name AFTER sound is mastered
4. Use multi-modal: visual + audio + mouth position

#### Mastery Criteria

A letter is "mastered" when child:
- ✓ Identifies correctly 3+ times in a row
- ✓ Can say the sound independently
- ✓ Can identify the sound in simple words
- ✓ Shows no hesitation on identification

### Word Families

**Word Family Teaching Strategy:**
Teach sight words in pattern groups to help children recognize word patterns.

**Example: -at Family**
- cat, hat, mat, sat, bat, rat, pat

**Example: -en Family**
- hen, pen, ten, men, den, then

**Example: -ig Family**
- pig, big, dig, fig, wig

**How to Apply:**
1. Show sight word from specific family
2. Highlight the word family pattern (-at, -en, -ig)
3. Show other words from same family
4. Reinforce pattern recognition

## 3. Fluency Pedagogy

### Grade-Level Norms (Research-Based)

**DIBELS / Hasbrouck & Tindal ORF Norms**

| Grade | Age | Spring Benchmark | Goal Range | Accuracy Target |
|-------|------|-----------------|--------------|----------------|
| Kindergarten | 5-6 | 30-40 WPM | 20-50 WPM | 90%+ |
| 1st Grade | 6-7 | 60-80 WPM | 50-90 WPM | 95%+ |
| 2nd Grade | 7-8 | 80-100 WPM | 70-110 WPM | 96%+ |

**Age-Based Targets (for ReadinConnect):**

**Age 4-5 (Pre-K/Kindergarten)**
- Practice: 10-20 WPM
- Good: 15-25 WPM
- Excellent: 20-30 WPM
- Passage length: 30-50 words
- Accuracy target: 85%+

**Age 5-6 (Kindergarten)**
- Practice: 15-25 WPM
- Good: 20-35 WPM
- Excellent: 25-40 WPM
- Passage length: 50-75 words
- Accuracy target: 90%+

**Age 6-7 (1st Grade)**
- Practice: 25-35 WPM
- Good: 35-50 WPM
- Excellent: 50-65 WPM
- Passage length: 75-100 words
- Accuracy target: 95%+

**Age 7-8 (2nd Grade)**
- Practice: 35-50 WPM
- Good: 50-75 WPM
- Excellent: 75-100 WPM
- Passage length: 100-150 words
- Accuracy target: 96%+

### Reading Verification

**Current Issue:** No verification child is actually reading

**Solution: Audio Recording + Speech-to-Text**

**Implementation:**
1. Child clicks "Start Reading"
2. App records audio continuously (via Web Speech API)
3. App converts speech to text in real-time
4. Compare spoken text with passage text
5. Count:
   - Total words read
   - Mispronunciations (detected by confidence score)
   - Skipped words (not spoken)
   - Added words (said but not in text)
6. Calculate:
   - WPM = (words read / minutes)
   - Accuracy = (correct words / total words) × 100

**Teacher Override Option:**
- Allow teacher to listen to recording
- Manual error adjustment if auto-detection is wrong
- Store recording for progress review

### Error Tracking

**Best Practices:**
- Self-corrected errors don't count
- Substitutions count as errors (e.g., said "cat" for "dog")
- Omissions count as errors (skipped word)
- Additions count as errors (said extra words)

**Research-Backed:**
- 3 or fewer self-corrections per 100 words = good
- 95%+ accuracy is target for all grade levels
- Both WPM and accuracy must be considered together

## 4. Comprehension Pedagogy

### QAR Framework (Question-Answer Relationships)

**Four Question Types:**

**1. Right There (Literal)**
- Question: What color was the cat?
- Passage: "The cat was black."
- Answer: Black
- Characteristic: Answer is directly stated in text

**2. Think and Search (Literal)**
- Question: What did the cat eat?
- Passage: "The cat was hungry. She looked for food and found a fish."
- Answer: A fish
- Characteristic: Answer requires combining information from different sentences

**3. Author and Me (Inferential)**
- Question: How did the cat feel?
- Passage: "The cat purred and rubbed against my leg."
- Answer: Happy (author's text + reader's inference)
- Characteristic: Reader must combine text with own thinking

**4. On My Own (Evaluative)**
- Question: Should cats be allowed inside?
- Passage: "Cats make good pets."
- Answer: (Reader's opinion based on background knowledge)
- Characteristic: Answer requires reader's own judgment

### Passage Difficulty

**Age-Appropriate Length and Complexity:**

**Age 4-5**
- Length: 30-50 words
- Sentences: 3-5
- Structure: Simple, one idea per sentence
- Vocabulary: High-frequency words, 1-2 new words max

**Age 5-6**
- Length: 50-75 words
- Sentences: 5-7
- Structure: Compound sentences, but clear cause-effect
- Vocabulary: Mostly familiar, 2-3 new words

**Age 6-7**
- Length: 75-100 words
- Sentences: 7-10
- Structure: Varied, some complex sentences
- Vocabulary: Mix of familiar and new, 3-5 new words

**Age 7-8**
- Length: 100-150 words
- Sentences: 10-15
- Structure: Complex, paragraphs, dialogue
- Vocabulary: Rich, 5-8 new words with context clues

### Comprehension Feedback

**Best Practices:**

**For Literal Questions:**
- Wrong: "The passage says: '[quote]'"
- Shows exactly where answer is found
- Helps child locate evidence

**For Inferential Questions:**
- Wrong: "The text clues: '[quote]' suggest that..."
- Points to evidence + explains inference
- Teaches reasoning process

**For Evaluative Questions:**
- Wrong: "Your opinion is valid because..."
- Doesn't say opinion is wrong
- Explains that opinions are personal

**For Correct Answers:**
- Always: "Great job! The evidence is: '[quote]'"
- Reinforces correct thinking process
- Points to supporting text

### Question Progression

**Level 1 (Literal Only)**
- 3 questions
- All "Right There" or "Think and Search"
- Focus on finding information in text

**Level 2 (Mixed)**
- 4 questions
- 2 literal + 2 inferential
- Starts building inference skills

**Level 3 (Complex)**
- 4-5 questions
- 1 literal + 2 inferential + 1-2 evaluative
- Full range of comprehension types

**Level 4 (Advanced)**
- 5 questions
- 1 literal + 1 inferential + 2-3 evaluative
- Emphasis on critical thinking

## 5. Cross-Game Principles

### Progress Persistence

**What to Track Per Game:**

**Phonics:**
- Mastered phonemes (by letter)
- Current phase/level
- Streaks
- Total attempts
- Accuracy rate

**Sight Words:**
- Mastered words (by list: pre-primer, primer, 1st grade, 2nd grade)
- Current level
- Longest streak
- Words encountered
- Accuracy rate

**Fluency:**
- Best WPM by passage
- Average WPM
- Average accuracy
- Passage completions
- Error patterns (specific errors repeated)

**Comprehension:**
- Passage completions
- Question type accuracy (literal, inferential, evaluative)
- Overall accuracy
- Current level
- Best score

### Age-Appropriate Content

**Why It Matters:**
- 4-year-olds and 8-year-olds have vastly different abilities
- Content must be challenging but not frustrating
- Progression must respect developmental stages

**Implementation:**
1. User registers with age
2. System sets "developmental stage"
3. All games load age-appropriate content
4. Difficulty adjusts within age range

**Developmental Stages:**

**Stage 1 (Age 4-5)**
- Focus: Letter recognition, very basic sight words, simple passages
- Game settings: Slower, more hints, easier targets

**Stage 2 (Age 5-6)**
- Focus: Phonics blends, basic sight words, longer passages
- Game settings: Moderate pace, moderate hints, moderate complexity

**Stage 3 (Age 6-7)**
- Focus: Complex phonics, advanced sight words, inferential comprehension
- Game settings: Normal pace, fewer hints, higher complexity

**Stage 4 (Age 7-8)**
- Focus: Advanced phonics, full sight word lists, evaluative comprehension
- Game settings: Faster pace, minimal hints, highest complexity
